## 4 The architecture to implement

Build one Kalshi-specific Python project with clear boundaries. Prefer local Parquet files for normalized tables and SQLite for task state and registries. Use immutable files for raw inputs and large artifacts. Pin dependency versions and random seeds. Choose a supported GRIB environment after auditing the collector rather than assuming that the existing Windows Python can load its native dependencies.

The acquisition adapter wraps the supplied collector and supplementary source clients. The normalizer turns provider formats into canonical records without discarding issue times, valid times, source versions, or quality flags. The dataset builder creates frozen chronological partitions. The forecasting layer produces distributions; the contract mapper converts those distributions into YES and NO probabilities.

The historical replay engine applies a fixed decision policy to time-ordered market evidence. It handles prices, costs, quantities, opportunity selection, hypothetical entries, and settlement. The evaluator computes metrics from saved predictions and ledger entries. The report generator explains results and limitations.

The research controller assigns bounded tasks to agents, validates their proposed actions, manages dependencies and budgets, and stores evidence. Workers create candidate implementations in isolated work areas. They cannot alter protected scoring code, change the research target, increase budgets, or access the final test labels.

The authoritative workflow is:

```text
Historical acquisition -> immutable raw files -> normalized tables
                                             -> frozen dataset partitions
Research task -> candidate code -> offline experiment runner
              -> predictions and replay ledger -> deterministic metrics
              -> evidence registry -> synthesis -> next research tasks
Frozen candidate -> separate final evaluator -> historical results report
```

Implement the minimum reliable version of each module before adding complexity. JSON schema validation, file hashes, transactional task state, and a small task queue are adequate foundations. A distributed platform, vector database, or custom general-purpose agent framework is not a prerequisite.

## 11 Forecast models and calibration

Define each feature at the simulated cutoff.

Use consistent decision times before and during the historical target day. Choose the candidate schedule before evaluating returns. Treat the schedule as a research parameter with a bounded search budget, not unlimited opportunities to select the most profitable minute after the fact.

Learn probability calibration within chronological training and validation. Check reliability overall, by forecast horizon, by probability range, and in the subset selected by the 10 percent rule. Avoid declaring calibration satisfactory from a handful of selected trades. GFS-derived products may share errors; estimate their incremental contribution rather than granting each an independent vote.

## 12 Return definition and historical replay

Let q be the estimated probability that the purchased side pays one dollar, c the executable entry price per contract, f allocated entry fees, and s any additional applicable settlement costs. The initial strategy buys contracts and holds to settlement. Its estimated net return on entry outlay is:

```text
expected_net_profit = q - c - f - s
expected_net_return = (q - c - f - s) / (c + f)
candidate_screen = expected_net_return >= 0.10
```

Use the YES probability for a YES purchase and its complement for NO. Apply actual size-dependent prices and fee rounding. Include spread and slippage in the modeled executable price and avoid counting them twice. A 10 percentage-point probability gap is not the same as 10 percent ROI.

For each hypothetical filled entry, compute historical simulated realized profit from the actual settlement payout less entry outlay and applicable costs. Report per-trade ROI and aggregate capital-weighted ROI as separate metrics. Aggregate ROI equals total simulated profit divided by total entry outlay; it is not an annual account return. A mean of individual trade ROIs answers a different question and must be labeled.

Implement an event-time replay with a frozen decision schedule, quote-age limit, order size, price convention, latency assumption, duplicate-entry rule, exposure cap and settlement policy. These are research settings, not permission to trade. Define conservative reference sizes and sensitivity cases before the campaign; do not quietly choose a size that improves reported returns.

Every decision record contains accepted or skipped status, reason, information cutoff, model and dataset versions, probability, costs, price source, size, expected ROI, and execution-evidence grade. The ledger links decisions to simulated entries and outcomes. Preserve zero-opportunity periods and losses. A strategy must be allowed to abstain when nothing meets the rule.

## 13 Execution evidence and honest gain reporting

Classify each replay scenario by the evidence supporting its hypothetical fill. **Grade A** uses sufficiently detailed historical quotes and sizes with a specified execution delay; it is an execution-aware simulation, still not proof of an actual fill. **Grade B** uses timestamped bid and ask summaries with limited depth or timing evidence, applies conservative assumptions, and reports an assumed-fill scenario. **Grade C** uses only prints or aggregated prices; it supports price-based sensitivity analysis but cannot substantiate an executable 10 percent edge.

Do not infer passive-order fills from a candle touching a limit. Queue priority, cancellations, available size, adverse selection, and event sequence matter. Exclude block trades from ordinary fill assumptions unless the strategy could have participated on equivalent terms. Never use the final settled market's displayed price as an earlier entry price.

If historical depth cannot be obtained, continue forecasting and limited price-based analysis, but mark execution adequacy as unresolved. A completed offline campaign may legitimately conclude that market execution evidence is insufficient. It may not label a weakly supported replay as proven realizable profit.

Run cost and timing sensitivity cases: historical fee schedule where available, present-cost alternative, wider spreads, delayed entry, stale quotes removed, and smaller available size. Freeze the cases before inspecting final results. Keep a count of how many qualifying opportunities disappear under each case.

Report net simulated PnL, entry outlay, trade count, independent weather-day count, wins and losses, average and capital-weighted returns, holding times, capacity, daily concentration, drawdown under an explicit capital model, and confidence intervals. Always show the selected date range and excluded-data ledger.

The term realized gains in this project means outcomes realized in a historical simulation from observed settlements. These are not gains earned in a brokerage account. The distinction must appear on every economic performance report. No numerical return result is currently established.

## 14 Evaluation and protection against overfitting

Select exact chronological partitions after the coverage audit and before discovery agents inspect evaluation outcomes. Prefer rolling-origin development folds, a separate chronological selection period, and a final protected test period. Keep all contracts and repeated forecasts from the same weather day together. Separate transformations, feature selection, calibration, and decision-rule fitting by fold.

A planning target is at least two years of usable overlapping inputs, with the latest protected interval long enough to cover meaningful variation. The actual final interval and minimum evidence requirements must be registered from data adequacy, not selected because its returns look favorable. If coverage is short, state the narrower inference and do not claim year-round robustness.

Use purging or temporal buffers when labels, forecast horizons or overlapping features can cross a partition boundary. Bootstrap or otherwise estimate uncertainty at the weather-day or suitable block level; thousands of related contracts are not thousands of independent weather events.

Log every hypothesis and parameter search, including failed attempts. Use the same score definitions across candidates. Compare the baseline, component changes, and combinations to measure incremental value. Evaluate the selected trading subset as well as the overall forecast distribution. Separate statistical evidence, practical significance, and claimed economic advantage.

The final evaluator receives one frozen candidate, code and data hashes, and locked settings. It calculates scores in a restricted process. Discovery agents do not receive final labels. Once final results influence development, the test is spent; future confirmation needs a new untouched period. Rerunning identical code to verify determinism is different from selecting another model against the same revealed outcomes.

Scientific outcomes are `SUPPORTED_WITH_LIMITATIONS`, `NO_IMPROVEMENT`, `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, or `INVALIDATED`. Positive historical realized ROI alone does not prove true expected ROI is at least 10 percent. The final report must distinguish the estimated selection threshold from the strength of evidence about future expectation.
